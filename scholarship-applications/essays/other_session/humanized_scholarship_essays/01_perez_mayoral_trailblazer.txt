Akhil Bodahanapati
University of Pittsburgh, Computer Science, Expected December 2027
Perez Mayoral Trailblazer Scholarship

Prompt: Describe your ultimate career or advocacy goals and how you envision making an impact in your community.

When people hear that I grew up in Cupertino, they often picture good schools, tech jobs, and a straight path to a computer science degree. That was part of my childhood, but it leaves out what was happening at home.

My family dealt with health problems that lasted for years. We moved more than once, so I had to adjust to new schools and start over with new people. Money became tight in ways that no one around us would have guessed. In a place like Cupertino, struggling can feel like something you are supposed to keep private.

The most exhausting part was the time we spent trying to understand systems that were supposed to help us. Who do you call? What does a letter mean? Which form is the right one, and what happens if you make a mistake? Medical, financial, and school systems all seemed to assume that people already knew how they worked. My family often had to learn the rules while we were already worn down.

That experience shaped how I think about advocacy. I think about the person sitting at a kitchen table at midnight, trying to understand a bill or denial letter and wondering whether there are any other options. People get lost in these systems because the information is buried, confusing, or written for someone else.

I study computer science at the University of Pittsburgh and expect to graduate in December 2027. I first liked computer science because code felt fair. A program runs or it does not, and when it fails, you can work through the problem and fix it. That sense of control mattered when other parts of my life felt unstable. Over time, I realized I could use the same skills to give other people some of that control.

I want to build software that helps people navigate systems they cannot avoid. I am interested in tools that explain medical bills and insurance decisions in plain language, help families find assistance programs they qualify for, or guide tenants and homeowners through their rights before a problem becomes a crisis. The technology to build these tools already exists. Too often, the missing piece is an engineer who understands what it feels like to be confused, tired, and afraid of getting the process wrong.

Your firm's work stood out to me because you represent individuals and small businesses in disputes with HOAs and insurance companies. Those organizations usually have more resources and a better understanding of the rules. I want to work on the same imbalance through software. A lawyer can protect someone from being steamrolled because they did not know their rights. I want to build tools that help people understand those rights earlier.

Right now, I am focused on finishing my degree well. My last semesters will be the most demanding part of the program, and the financial pressure my family has faced has not disappeared. This scholarship would give me more time for coursework and less time worrying about how to pay for it.

After college, I want to work on software for healthcare billing, insurance, benefits, and housing. I would bring the perspective of someone who has struggled through complicated systems without much guidance. I also want to be someone that family members, friends, and younger students can come to when they are stuck on the same kinds of problems I once handled alone. If my work gives the next person at that kitchen table clearer information and a better idea of what to do, I will have built the kind of career I want.
